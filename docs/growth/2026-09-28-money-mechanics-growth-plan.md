## 2. What actually drives Shorts distribution

This section replaced my own assumptions. It is the evidence base for every recommendation later.

### 2.1 YouTube's official signal list is much narrower than creator advice assumes

[R] From YouTube's Shorts discovery page (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts):

> "% of viewers who chose to view, avg. view duration and avg. % viewed to inform ranking"
> "whether or not viewers enjoyed the Short using likes and post-watch survey results"

So the confirmed chain is: **impression → chose to view → avg % viewed / avg view duration → likes + satisfaction surveys.**

**[R] Not named as Shorts ranking signals:** comments, shares, rewatches/loops, subscriber count, video length, posting frequency, posting time, hashtags. The same page states there is "no minimum posting cadence required for your videos to perform well."

**[J] Three of the four confirmed signals are retention-shaped. That is where the work goes. Everything else is secondary at best.**

### 2.2 The metric to run the channel on

[R] In YouTube Studio (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts):

> **"How many chose to view** — Highlights the percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away."

**YouTube publishes no benchmark for it.** [R] The only real numbers come from Paddy Galloway & Chris Gileta's study of **5,400 Shorts across 33 channels, 3.3 billion views** (https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/):

- Best performers: **70–90% viewed**
- **Below 60% viewed = poor performance**

**[J] Use >70% as the target and <60% as a failed Short — while remembering this is April 2023 data, from before 3-minute Shorts and before both view-counting changes. It is the best number that exists, not a good one.**

**[R] The popular "swipe-away under 30% gets 4× the distribution" claim has no disclosed dataset and traces to marketing blogs. Unproven — do not plan against it as fact.**

### 2.3 ⚠️ Correction: there is no "test pool ceiling"

**I assumed the ~1,300 wall was a first test pool. The evidence does not support that.**

[R] YouTube describes a **continuous** evaluation, not staged waves: "when a Short is recommended to a viewer – do they choose to watch it, ignore it, or click 'not interested'? Then, we look at whether or not viewers stick around." No stages, no thresholds, no wave sizes (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts). The "seeds to 1,000–3,000 people" figure appears **only** in marketing blogs. **Unproven.**

**[R] Here is the reframe, and it matters.** The academic study of **9.9M Shorts and 6.9M regular videos across 70,000 channels** (Violot, Elmas, Bilogrevic & Humbert — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00454) found:

- **The median Short gets 1,986 views**
- **1% of Shorts capture 63% of all Shorts views**

**So this channel's ~1,400 wall is roughly the platform median for a Short.** [M+R] There is no gate to escape. There is an extreme power law, and Money Mechanics is sitting slightly below its middle.

**[J] Why this changes the plan:** "escape the pool" implies a threshold you cross once. "Land in the top few percent" implies repeated attempts with a low hit rate. The second is true. That is why §5 gates on the **median and the feed-decision rate** (things you can steadily improve) rather than only on a breakout (which is partly luck), and why the honest Day-90 breakout target is 3 videos, not 30.

### 2.4 ⚠️ Correction: length should be 60–90 seconds

I initially planned 30–45s. Three pieces of evidence point the other way, and one of them is decisive.

1. **Decisive [R]: below 60 seconds you get no retention curve at all.** The audience retention report requires a video "at least 60 seconds long and have at least 100 views", and its earliest checkpoint is *30 seconds*, not 3 (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop). **A 36-second Short — which is what the current best video is [M] — is undiagnosable.** You cannot see where people leave.
2. **[R] The only large length study points up.** Paddy Galloway's 5,400 Shorts found **50–60s was the peak-views bucket** (~1.7M average views), and the >50s average-view-duration bucket averaged 4.1M views vs 1.8M for 40–50s. *Caveat: 2023, when 60s was the cap — so "50–60s wins" was a finding at the ceiling and says nothing about 60–180s.*
3. **[R] Stay at or under 90s for music licensing:** "You can use most songs for up to 90 seconds in a Short up to 3 minutes. However, some tracks may be limited to 60 or 30 seconds" (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en).

**[J] 60–90 seconds. Not because a 2026 study proves it beats 30s — no such study exists — but because it is the only band where you can measure, license, and where the available evidence leans.**

### 2.5 The "3-second swipe" signal is folklore; the hook still matters

**[R] There is no published per-second early-drop-off signal for Shorts.** YouTube's tracked signal is binary at the impression level — viewed or swiped. And as above, you cannot even see 3-second retention on a sub-60s Short.

**[J] So why does §6 still spend most of its effort on the first three seconds?** Because the *feed decision* is officially confirmed, and it is made in the first moment. [R] Rene Ritchie's on-record framing: "The first act has to be a reward. The thumbnail makes a promise, and the video has to deliver on it" and "You can't do the slow burn… because people will click away" (https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/).

**Hook work is justified by a confirmed signal. "YouTube tracks your 3-second swipe rate" is not a real metric — stop using that language.**

### 2.6 Likes yes, comments no

[R] Likes are a **confirmed** ranking input. Comments are **not named** anywhere in the Shorts signal list.

[R] And comments are structurally scarce on Shorts. CreatorDB's study of **1,466,581 channels** plus a 480-channel within-channel panel (https://creatordb.app/research/youtube-view-count-change/) found:

- Median Shorts engagement rate **2.59%**
- **Likes per view: 98% of long-form levels** — essentially parity
- **Comments per view: 31% of long-form levels** — the entire Shorts engagement gap is comments
- Shorts engagement is **nearly flat across channel size** (2.2%–2.7% from 10k to 1M+ subs)

**[J] Two consequences. First: being small is not suppressing this channel's engagement rate — the 1,400 wall is not a subscriber penalty. Second: do not trade retention for comment-bait. Retention is confirmed; comments are not. Want comments for community and topic signal, not for reach.**

### 2.7 ⚠️ The category tailwind is not on this channel's side

**[R] This is the most strategically important finding in the research.** The same 9.9M-Short study measured the Shorts-to-regular-video view ratio by category:

| Category | Shorts advantage |
|---|---|
| Entertainment | **4.33 – 5.70** |
| **Education** | **3.28 – 4.26** (weakest of the advantaged categories) |
| Music, Film & Animation | 0.42 – 2.41 (long-form preferred) |

Their explicit finding: the Shorts-vs-long-form gap **narrows in education and political categories** — Shorts underperform there relative to other areas. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00454

**[J] The implication is not "abandon history". It is that history Shorts must win on entertainment-grade mechanics — hook, pace, narrative tension — because the structural tailwind entertainment Shorts enjoy is not there. Informational value alone will not carry a history Short. This is the reason §6 is about storytelling craft and not about facts per second.**

### 2.8 Loops: cheap, but unproven

[R] Rewatches are **not** in the published signal list. And YouTube stated plainly that the August 2026 counting change — which made loops inflate raw view counts — did **not** change recommendations:

> "No change to recommendations — the algorithm." — https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/

That page also defines the distinction that matters: a **view** counts "from the very first frame", while an **engaged view** means "clicking to watch or watching past the initial seconds. This has been, and will continue to be, how you qualify to earn."

**[J] A loop ending plausibly lifts avg % viewed, which *is* a confirmed signal. That is a legitimate mechanism. "Loops double your distribution" is unproven. Keep loop endings because they are free; do not restructure storytelling around them.**

### 2.9 Frequency and timing — and an honest contradiction

There is a real tension in the evidence and I am not going to paper over it.

**Against volume [M]:** my own competitor pulls show frequency does not raise *per-video* views. Simple History posts nearly **daily** with **5.11M subs** and has a median of **20,500**. Knowledgia posted **once every 12 days**, has less than half the subscribers, and has a median of **1,800,000** — 88× higher. And the owner measured that a 15-Short burst over two days did worst. [M]

**For volume [R]:** vidIQ's study of **10,210,278 channels** (https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study/) found monthly view growth rising monotonically with cadence — **2.18% at ≥12 uploads/month vs 0.53% at <1/month, a 4.1× gap** — and subscriber growth from 0.00% to 0.91%. Metricool's study of **799,718 videos across 71,177 accounts** found Shorts monthly view totals keep rising with frequency with no penalty, and that **accounts under 10,000 subscribers grew Shorts views over 200%** (https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/).

**[J] Both are true because they measure different things.** Frequency raises *total channel views*; it does not raise the *median per video*. Since this pipeline is automated, one Short a day is nearly free — so keep it. But: never respond to a weak median by posting more, and never burst. The owner's own data already settled the burst question. [M]

**Timing: [R] unknowable from public data, and the published numbers are mutually inconsistent.** The widely-quoted Metricool "2–6 p.m." window is misreported — their own page says most Shorts in the study "went up" between 2 and 6 p.m., which is **when creators post, not when Shorts perform** (https://metricool.com/youtube-shorts-algorithm/). **[J] Fix one time slot, and stop thinking about it. You cannot get a clean read at one upload a day.**

### 2.10 One opportunity worth chasing immediately

[R] At Made on YouTube on **2026-09-23** — five days ago — YouTube announced **A/B testing of up to three different video cuts, explicitly including Shorts, judged on watch-time retention curves**, rolling out to select creators with global expansion intended next year (https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/made-on-youtube-new-tools-power-creation-journey/ · mechanics: https://ppc.land/youtube-to-test-up-to-3-versions-of-one-video-on-real-viewers-shorts-too/).

**[J] This is the first legitimate way to test hooks on real Shorts viewers, and it is aimed at exactly this channel's problem. Apply for access now. It is also the highest-leverage app request in §11.**

## 4. Decision: keep the channel, rename it

**Recommendation: KEEP the existing channel. Rename it. Unlist the finance catalogue. Do NOT start a clean channel.**

### Why not start clean

A new channel costs the only proven asset there is: **14 Shorts that reached 900–1,415 views on history topics** [M]. Small, but real evidence of what works, attached to this channel. You would also discard channel age for nothing.

### Why renaming is free

[R] A rename keeps subscribers, views, analytics and YPP status. The only documented side effect is losing a verification badge — and this channel has none:

> "You can change your channel name twice within a 14-day period" · "Changing your name will remove your verification badge."
> — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2657964?hl=en

**[J] So renaming buys the clean-channel benefit — name matches content — at zero cost.**

### Why the finance back catalogue must go — the real risk

[R] Monetization review is **channel-wide**, and reviewers look at theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, and the About section:

> "Our reused content policy applies to your channel as a whole. If you have videos that violate our guidelines… monetization may be removed from your entire channel."
> "our reviewers may focus on your channel's: Main theme / Most viewed videos / Newest videos / Biggest proportion of watch time / Video metadata (including titles, thumbnails, and descriptions) / Channel's "About" section"
> — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en

[R] And a section added in July 2026 names finance directly:

> "**AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics.** This policy refers to channels that use AI-generated personas to deliver information on sensitive topics. This includes any content that presents itself as a human expert providing advice to viewers on topics such as health, legal issues, **finances**, or politics… channels uploading this content will not be allowed to monetize."

## 5. The 90-day plan

Fixed across all phases: **one Short per day, 60–90 seconds, one fixed upload time, history only.** Rationale in §2.4 (length), §2.9 (frequency and timing).

### Phase 1 — Days 1–30 (Sep 29 → Oct 28): fix the hook, name the subject, get measurable

**Publish:** 1 Short/day, **60–90s** (this alone is a change from the current 36s — and it is what makes retention diagnosable at all, §2.4).

- Every video must have a subject the viewer already knows **by name**, and the name must be in the title **and** in the first spoken line.
- Rotate the four proven archetypes from §3 — counterfactual question, myth correction, superlative claim, answer-forward promise. Roughly 7–8 of each across the month.
- Kill the invented-character fables entirely. [M] They got 1–6 views.
- Re-make 5 proven topics (Marcus Aurelius, Berlin Conference, Haiti indemnity, the Baltic Way, Scotland's Darién bankruptcy) with new scripts.
- **Reset the baseline.** [R] Pre-August-2026 view numbers are not comparable (§0).

**Measure daily:** **"how many chose to view"** · avg % viewed · avg view duration · views at 24h and 72h · like rate. Tag every video with its hook archetype so archetypes can be compared at day 30.

**Go/no-go gate (day 30):**
- **"Chose to view" above 70% on at least half the month's Shorts** (§2.2 — the primary gate, because it is a confirmed signal you can act on)
- **At least 3 Shorts above 3,000 views**
- **Median above 1,200**

> If you miss it: the problem is topic choice or the opening. **Do not increase volume. Do not start long-form.** Repeat Phase 1 with only the best-performing archetype.

### Phase 2 — Days 31–60 (Oct 29 → Nov 27): find the ceiling, build a reason to subscribe

**Publish:** 1 Short/day, only the archetypes that won in Phase 1.

- **Length test inside the band:** two weeks at 60–75s, two weeks at 75–90s. Compare avg % viewed and total views. [J] You now have retention curves, so this test is readable — it would not have been at 36s.
- **Start one named series** (e.g. "Empires that broke themselves", one entry per week). **[J] A series is the only honest reason a stranger subscribes.** At 0.099% subs/view [M] subscriber conversion is the binding constraint, and it is the one thing content design moves directly.
- **Apply for the video A/B testing beta** (§2.10) and use it on hooks if granted.

**Measure:** the Phase 1 set, plus subs-per-view per video, plus retention curve shape by length.

**Go/no-go gate (day 60):** **median above 2,000** · **at least 2 Shorts above 10,000 views** · **150+ subscribers** · **"chose to view" above 70% on most Shorts**

> If you miss the 10,000 gate but the median rose: keep going, do not start long-form yet. If the median did not rise either, stop and reconsider the niche.

### Phase 3 — Days 61–90 (Nov 28 → Dec 27): restart long-form off proven topics

**Publish:** 1 Short/day **+ 1 long video/week** (4 total).

- Every long video expands a Short that **already passed 10,000 views**. No exceptions.
- Length **8–12 minutes** (History Matters median is 524s = 8.7 min at a 620,000 median [M]).

**Measure:** long-form avg view duration and avg % viewed · click-through rate · traffic source split (Shorts feed vs browse vs suggested) · watch hours accumulating toward 3,000.

**Go/no-go gate (day 90):** **250+ subscribers** · **3,000 views/day** · **1 long video above 1,000 views with 40%+ average percentage viewed**

## 6. Shorts playbook

### The first 3 seconds

Justified by the **feed decision**, which is a confirmed signal (§2.5) — not by a mythical 3-second metric.

1. **Speak the title as the first line.** [M] Knowledgia's 10M and 7.1M Shorts do exactly this.
2. **Name the famous thing in the first four words.** [M] The channel's obscure-subject videos died (Zheng He 230, Aztecs 35).
3. **Open a loop, do not announce a fact.** A question or a wrong belief gives a reason to stay. "Did you know" does not. [J]
4. **[R] Give a reward, not a set-up** — "The first act has to be a reward… You can't do the slow burn" (Ritchie, https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/).

**10 openings written for this channel** — say these as the first line, with the same words on the hook card:

1. "Why didn't Rome ever conquer Scotland?"
2. "Rome's wisest emperor picked the worst possible heir."
3. "Why wasn't Ethiopia colonised when every other African country was?"
4. "Napoleon sold America a third of its land — because he lost Haiti."
5. "Spain received more silver than any empire in history, and went bankrupt."
6. "The Mongols were winning in Europe. Then they went home to vote."
7. "You think the Titanic broke the rules. It didn't — it followed them."
8. "Britain's biggest company had its own army of 260,000 men."
9. "Fourteen men in a Berlin room drew the borders of Africa in 1884."
10. "Two million people held hands, and the Soviet Union ended."

Nine of ten name a famous subject in the first four words. All ten open a loop. None says "Did you know". [J]

### Title rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Put the famous name in the title: **"Marcus Aurelius"**, not "The Wisest Roman Emperor" [M] | Hide the subject behind a description |
| Use the question form where it fits — the top comparables live on it [M] | "You won't believe", "shocking", "insane" |
| Match the title to the first spoken line **word for word** [M] | Write a title the script never says |
| 5–9 words | Emoji. [M] The channel's top Shorts use them and still capped at 1,400 — no evidence they help, and they cost title space |
| Keep the title identical across a Short and its long version | `#shorts` stuffing — unnecessary, eats the title |

### On-screen text

- Hook card: **2–6 words** over the first 3s (already built). It must carry **subject + tension**: "ROME'S WORST HEIR", not "HISTORY FACT".
- Captions on, full width, one line at a time, never covering a face.
- One idea on screen at a time.

### Length

**60–90 seconds.** Full reasoning in §2.4: below 60s there is no retention curve, above 90s music licensing breaks, and the only large study points upward. **[J] This is the single biggest mechanical change to the current output (now 36s [M]).**

### Endings

Alternate (already built), with intent:

- **Loop close** — end on the sentence that makes the opening question make sense, so a replay reads naturally. **[J] Cheap and plausibly lifts avg % viewed. Do not believe it doubles distribution (§2.8).**
- **Open question close** — end on the genuinely unresolved part.
- **Consequence close** — end on what the event caused, and name it.

Never "follow for more" or "part 2 in the comments". [J]

### Comments — worth having, not worth optimising for

**[R] Comments are not a named Shorts ranking signal, and comments-per-view on Shorts run at 31% of long-form levels (§2.6). There is no credible evidence that raising comment rate raises reach.**

**[J] So: want comments as community and topic-demand signal, never at the cost of retention.** Four mechanisms that fit history and cost nothing:

1. **Leave one detail unexplained** and let people ask.
2. **Name a disputed point honestly** — "historians still argue about whether he meant it."
3. **Invite a comparison, not an opinion** — "no other empire did this" and someone will name one.
4. **Be correctable.** A precise date invites precise replies. Highest-yield, and only works if the research is real.

[M] Current rate: 0–3 comments per video; best observed 2 on 1,415 views (0.14%). History Matters gets 0.27% on long-form [M]. Headroom exists, but it is a small lever.

## 7. Long-form playbook

Long-form is **mandatory**, because of §1: the fan-funding tier via **3,000 watch hours** is the only monetization gate this channel can realistically reach. [R] And Shorts time does not count toward it:

> "any qualified watch hours from Shorts views in the Shorts Feed won't count towards the 4,000 qualified public watch hours threshold"
> — https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en

**[R] There is a second reason, from §2.7:** education content shows the *weakest* Shorts advantage of any advantaged category (3.28–4.26 vs entertainment's 4.33–5.70). **[J] Informational content is structurally better suited to long-form than to Shorts. This channel's niche wants long videos more than the average channel does.**

### What 3,000 hours actually costs

| Gate | Hours | Minutes | Long-form views at 4 min average watched |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fan funding (500 subs)** | 3,000 | 180,000 | **45,000** |
| Full YPP now (1,000 subs) | 4,000 | 240,000 | 60,000 |
| Full YPP from 2027-02-01 | 8,000 | 480,000 | 120,000 |

[M] arithmetic; [R] thresholds. A 10-minute video holding 40% gives 4 minutes watched.

**[M] The current 12 long videos get 2–20 views each.** Restarting long-form now produces nothing. Hence the gate.

### When to restart: the trigger

**Restart long-form only when one Short has passed 10,000 views AND the channel has 150+ subscribers.** [J]

Below 10,000 views on a Short there is no evidence any topic has demand; below ~150 subs there is nobody to show a new long video to on day one. [M] The existing 2–20 view long videos are what publishing without either looks like.

### Rules

| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length | **8–12 min** | [M] History Matters median 524s → 620K median views |
| Frequency | **1/week max.** Knowledgia at 1 per 12 days and History Matters at 1 per 17 days both beat daily posters on median | [M] |
| Topic choice | **Only expand a Short that beat 10,000 views.** Never guess | [J] |
| Title | Same question form, same famous name, same words the Short used | [M] |
| Thumbnail | One subject + **3–5 words maximum**. The words must not repeat the title — they add the turn | [J] |

### How the two formats feed each other

- **Short → long:** the Short is the demand test. Only proven topics get 9 minutes.
- **Long → Short:** cut 2–3 new Shorts from each long video's strongest moments. **Rewrite the hook — do not clip narration straight out.**
- **Link them:** long video in the Short's pinned comment and description. **[J] Expect a low click rate. Small gain, not a strategy.**
- **[J] One warning, and it is real:** [R] one dataset found months with Shorts activity showed lower long-form RPM in Entertainment (−60%) and Kids & Teens (−26%), and its own authors "cannot determine" whether Shorts cause it (https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026). **Education was not in that analysis, so this may not apply to history at all.** Watch long-form RPM once monetized; do not act on it before then.

Either way: Shorts money is negligible.**

**Long-form, Education & Science** — the closest proxy; **there is no "History" niche in any dataset I found** [R] (https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm):

## 10. Risks

### 1. Inauthentic content policy — highest risk, and the format *is* the risk

[R] Renamed from "repetitious content" on 2025-07-15 (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en):

> "July 15, 2025: We're making a minor update to our "repetitious content" policy to better clarify this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced. We are also renaming this policy from "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content.""

The sentences that describe this pipeline almost exactly [R], same page:

> "**Image slideshows**, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
> "AI-generated content made with **generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production** without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective"
> "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value… or **minimal variation across videos**"

**But AI is explicitly not the trigger** [R], same page:

> "Content that expresses your unique creative voice, like using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented"
> "If you use automated tools or templates to help create your content, the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision and provide educational or entertainment value."
> "What's important is that the substance of each video should be materially varied and deliver creative, educational, or other value."

**What to do:**
- **Wire the paid image-to-video.** [J] "Generated stills with a slow pan" is close to the literal words "image slideshows". Motion is the cheapest distance you can put between your output and that sentence. **Highest-value engineering item.**
- **Vary substance deliberately.** Rotate era, region and story structure so no two consecutive videos feel interchangeable.
- **Write original scripts from multiple sources**, not paraphrased Wikipedia. Keep the research notes.
- **Write a real About section.** [R] Reviewers read it.

### 2. Reused content — a separate, additional test

[R] Same page:

> "Reused content refers to channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value."
> Not allowed: "Content that **exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create**, like text from websites or news feeds"

**[J] This is why original writing is load-bearing, not optional.** An AI reading a rewritten encyclopedia entry over generated pictures is the thing that sentence describes. The defence is genuine synthesis — a claim the script makes that no single source makes. Store a source list per video so you can prove it.

### 3. AI disclosure — ambiguous, so disclose

[R] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en requires disclosure when AI "meaningfully alter[s] or generate[s] photorealistic content", including content that "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur". Exempt: scripts, thumbnails, outlines, cloning your own voice, captions, upscaling, idea generation.

**[J] Photorealistic generated images of real historical events sit right on that line, and the official examples do not cover historical reconstruction. This is a genuine gap in the rules.** Disclose — it is cheap:

[R] > "a disclosure label alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it's eligible to earn money" — https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/

[R] And from May 2026 YouTube labels automatically anyway: > "Starting in May 2026, we're rolling out new internal signals to help identify AI-generated content." Self-disclosing costs nothing and avoids a wrong automatic call.

### 4. Finance back catalogue

Unlist it this week — see §4. [R] The "AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics" section names finance advice explicitly, and review is channel-wide.

### 5. Enforcement evidence is much weaker than the internet claims

**[J] Do not plan around scare numbers.** The widely-repeated claim that YouTube terminated 16 channels with 35M subscribers in January 2026 appears in reporting (https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage) but **names no channel and has no traceable primary source.** A "130,000 channels in six months" figure traces to a Google *research paper* of unconfirmed deployment (https://fstoppers.com/artificial-intelligence/how-google-machine-terminated-130000-ai-slop-youtube-channels-six-months-903645), which that article itself cautions about. **There is no verified public dataset of AI-faceless demonetizations.** Design against the policy text, which is real, and ignore the rest.

### 6. Burnout of one format

**[M] Volume is not what raises per-video views** — Simple History posts daily at 5.11M subs for a 20,500 median; Knowledgia posted every 12 days for a 1,800,000 median. So the mitigation is not "post less"; it is variety within one upload a day: four hook archetypes, three ending types, rotating eras and regions.

**[J] If the median stops moving for 30 straight days, the format is exhausted. Change the format, not the frequency.**

### 7. Nobody is watching the robot

[M] The pipeline publishes unattended at 02:03. One bad script, one wrong date, one offensive framing ships with no human in between. On a channel facing channel-wide monetization review, a single bad upload is a **channel-level** risk. **[J] Add a hold-for-review queue. Cheapest risk reduction available.**

### 8. Broken baselines

[R] View counting changed on 2025-03-31 and 2026-08-24 (§0). **[J] Any "we used to average X" comparison spanning those dates is meaningless. Reset the baseline on day 1 and only compare within the new regime.**

## 12. What I could not establish

Stated plainly, so nobody builds on sand:

1. **No "History" RPM figure exists** in any dataset. Education & Science is a proxy, not a measurement.
2. **No Shorts RPM for education exists** — the best dataset has zero education Shorts channels.
3. **No verified case** of a named AI faceless channel demonetized under the inauthentic-content policy. Circulating numbers have no primary source.
4. **Whether photorealistic AI images of real historical events require disclosure** is genuinely ambiguous in the official rules.
5. **No credible study compares sub-30s against 60–180s Shorts** under the 3-minute regime. The 60–90s recommendation rests on measurability, licensing and a 2023 study — not on direct proof.
6. **No study compares steady cadence against bursty cadence at matched volume.** "One a day beats bursts" rests on this channel's own single observation [M], not on published evidence.
7. **Whether comment rate predicts Shorts reach** — no credible evidence either way; comments are not a named signal.
8. **Whether posting time matters.** [J] Fix one slot and stop thinking about it.
9. **Whether Shorts suppress long-form RPM for education channels** — the only study covered other niches and declines to claim causality.
10. **Why Knowledgia stopped posting Shorts in May 2025** [M] after a 1,800,000 median. I do not know, and it is worth knowing.
