# Money Mechanics — Revenue Plan

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Channel:** Money Mechanics — faceless finance and business explainers
**Operator:** single, Egypt-based, US audience target

> This document is deliberately unflattering in places. A plan that only describes the
> good outcome is not a plan. Figures are industry ranges, not guarantees, and should be
> re-checked against your own analytics once data exists.

---

## 1. The honest baseline

Most YouTube channels never reach monetization. The threshold is 1,000 subscribers **and**
4,000 watch hours in 12 months — or 1,000 subscribers and 10M Shorts views in 90 days.
The large majority of new channels never clear it, and automated channels face additional
scrutiny under YouTube's inauthentic-content policy.

So the first question is not "how much will this make" but "will this be monetized at all."
Everything below is conditional on clearing that bar.

**What is genuinely in your favour:**
- Cost per video is ~$1, against $50–500 for a human-produced equivalent. You can afford to
  be wrong for a long time.
- Finance is the highest-RPM niche on the platform.
- Consistency is the strongest known growth predictor, and consistency is exactly what an
  automated pipeline is good at.

**What is genuinely against you:**
- Finance is the most competitive niche, precisely because of the RPM.
- "Your Money or Your Life" content receives extra scrutiny.
- Automated + finance is the single most-demonetized combination on the platform.

The strategy that survives all three is quality per video, not volume.

## 2. Revenue streams, in the order they arrive

| # | Stream | Starts | Realistic scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AdSense** | after monetization | $10–25 RPM in this niche |
| 2 | **Affiliates** | ~5k subscribers | often exceeds AdSense early |
| 3 | **Sponsorships** | ~20–30k subscribers | the largest lever by far |
| 4 | **Own product** | ~50k subscribers | highest margin, most work |

### AdSense
Passive and automatic once approved. Pays on long-form watch time; Shorts pay roughly
100× less per view and exist to feed subscribers, not revenue.

### Affiliates
For this niche: brokerages, budgeting and research tools, book links. Commissions are high.
**Two hard rules** — disclose every affiliate link on screen and in the description, and
never promote a product you would not use. Finance audiences punish this faster than any
other, and YouTube treats undisclosed paid promotion as a policy violation.

### Sponsorships — where the money actually is
A finance channel with ~50k views per video can typically charge **$1,000–2,500** for an
integrated 60-second read. That is one sponsor slot earning more than a month of AdSense at
the same size. Sponsors buy audience *quality*, not raw view count, which is why niche
discipline matters more than growth rate.

### Own product
A course, template pack, or newsletter. Best margin, but it is a second business. Not before
50k subscribers, and only if the audience is asking for it.

## 3. What the numbers actually look like

Assumptions: 3 long videos/week (~13/month), $12 RPM, ~$70/month running cost.

| Stage | Monthly long-form views | AdSense | Sponsorship | Total/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Break-even | 6,000 | ~$72 | — | **~$0 net** |
| Early traction | 50,000 | ~$600 | — | **~$530 net** |
| Established | 200,000 | ~$2,400 | ~$1,500 | **~$3,800 net** |
| Strong | 500,000 | ~$6,000 | ~$4,000 | **~$9,900 net** |

**Break-even is about 6,000 long-form views per month.** That is a low bar — roughly 460
views per video. It is realistically reachable within 3–6 months of consistent publishing.

**"A lot of money" — being specific.** $5,000/month means roughly **250,000 long-form views
per month**, sustained. That is a genuinely successful channel, typically **18–24 months** of
consistent work, and most channels never get there. It is achievable. It is not quick, and
nothing about automation makes it quick — automation lowers your cost per attempt, not the
number of attempts required.

## 4. Quality bar — the thing that decides all of the above

Automation makes it trivially easy to publish 50 mediocre videos. That is the failure mode,
not the success case. YouTube's 2025 inauthentic-content policy targets exactly that, and
demonetization is retroactive across the whole channel, not per video.

**Non-negotiable per video:**

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The first 5 seconds pose a specific question. Retention is decided here. |
| **Sources** | Every factual claim cited on screen and listed in the description. |
| **Original angle** | A connection or framing not already in the top 3 results for that topic. |
| **No advice** | Explain how things work. Never recommend, predict, or promise returns. |
| **Human review** | Gate 2 is permanent. Watch every video before it publishes. |
| **Thumbnail** | Made deliberately, tested against alternatives. It decides whether anything else matters. |

**Publish 3 good videos a week, never 10 weak ones.** If a script fails Gate 1, discard it —
that costs $0.03. A weak video costs channel-level trust, which is not recoverable at any price.

## 5. Milestones

| Phase | Target | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Months 1–3** | 40 videos published | Find what works. Expect the first 20 to be weak; publish anyway, study retention. |
| **2 — Months 4–6** | 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours | Apply for monetization. Double down on whatever formats worked in phase 1. |
| **3 — Months 7–12** | 50k views/month | Add affiliates. First AdSense revenue. Improve thumbnails relentlessly. |
| **4 — Year 2** | 200k+ views/month | Approach sponsors. This is where income becomes meaningful. |

**The measure that matters in phase 1 is not views — it is average view duration.** Views
follow retention with a lag. A video with 500 views and 60% retention is a better signal than
one with 5,000 views and 20%.

## 6. Risks, and what to do about each

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Demonetized for automated content | Gate 2 permanent; sourcing on every claim; 3/week not 30 |
| Finance advice policy strike | Explainers only. No picks, predictions, or promises. |
| Channel never reaches 1,000 subs | Shorts cut from each long video — cheap, and they count toward the threshold |
| Running costs outpace revenue | ~$70/month is roughly one takeaway meal a week. Sustainable for a year. |
| AdSense payment issues from Egypt | Real name and address, W-8BEN filed correctly. Never misstate country. |
| Burnout | The pipeline removes production labour, not editorial judgement. Budget ~1 hour per video for the two gates. |

## 7. What actually decides this

Not the model choice. Not the render quality. Not the automation.

**Topic selection and thumbnails decide 80% of the outcome.** A superb script on a topic
nobody searches for earns nothing. A mediocre script on a well-chosen topic with a strong
thumbnail earns. Framecast makes production cheap so your time goes to the two things it
cannot automate: choosing what to make, and judging whether it is good enough to publish.
